# 17 — Grant proposals and funding

## Before writing
Read the current solicitation, not a summary. Record: programme officer, deadline, eligibility,
page limits per section, font/margin rules, required forms, review criteria and weights,
budget cap, indirect cost rules, letter-of-intent requirement, data-management and
open-access mandates, and the allowed use of generative AI in the application.

## Universal structure
1. **Specific aims / summary** — one page: problem, gap, hypothesis, aims, expected outcomes,
   impact. Numbers and feasibility, not adjectives.
2. **Significance** — why the problem matters, what is missing, who benefits.
3. **Innovation** — what is genuinely new (method, data, theory), with evidence that it is
   new.
4. **Approach** — aims → hypotheses → design → analysis → expected results → pitfalls and
   alternatives → timeline → milestones. Each aim must be independent enough that failure of
   one does not sink the whole proposal.
5. **Feasibility** — preliminary data, team expertise, resources, letters of support, data
   access agreements.
6. **Broader impacts / impact statement** — concrete activities with measurable outcomes,
   not generic outreach promises.
7. **Budget and justification** — line items tied to aims; personnel effort in person-months.
8. **Data management / sharing plan**, reproducibility, ethics and safety protocols.
9. **Biosketches, facilities, collaborators, letters** in the agency's exact format.

## Agency notes (verify current rules)
| Agency | Distinctive requirement |
|---|---|
| NSF | Broader impacts reviewed as a criterion; Project Description ~15 pages; no preliminary-data requirement in some programmes. |
| NIH | Specific Aims page, Significance/Innovation/Approach, rigor & transparency, authentication of key resources, inclusion across the lifespan. |
| DOE | Research narrative tied to programme priorities; computational/energy context. |
| DARPA | Technical Area breakdown, milestones, go/no-go metrics, Intellectual Property plan. |
| NSTC (Taiwan) | CM03 forms, Chinese/English naming, specific budget categories. |
| ERC / Horizon | Excellence and "beyond state of the art", methodology and work packages. |

## Reviewer psychology
Reviewers read many proposals quickly. Make the first paragraph of each section self-contained,
use informative headings that state the conclusion, put numbers in bold where the format
allows, and provide a visual timeline. Every aim gets a falsifiable milestone and a fallback
plan.

## Common rejection reasons
- No clear hypothesis; aims are a list of activities.
- Innovation claimed but not demonstrated against prior work.
- Underpowered or infeasible design; no pilot data where needed.
- Budget not tied to the work; missing cost-sharing approvals.
- Broader impacts that read as boilerplate.
- Overpromising deliverables; no risk mitigation.
- Non-compliance with formatting (immediate desk reject).

## Integrity
Never invent preliminary results, letters of support, collaborator commitments, institutional
resources or budget figures. Mark `TO CONFIRM` and assign a human owner for each missing item
before submission.
