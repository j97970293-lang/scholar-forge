# Introduction
Prior work reports that retrieval augmentation can reduce citation errors (Smith and Doe, 2020).
Verification benchmarks show that error rates differ substantially between corpora (Lee and Chen, 2021).
We therefore ask whether a retrieval-assisted writing workflow produces fewer unverifiable
references than an unassisted workflow on a small set of manuscript sections.

# Methods
We compared two writing workflows on 40 manuscript sections. The primary outcome was the number
of unverifiable references per 1,000 words, with bootstrap confidence intervals. Two annotators
independently checked each reference and disagreements were adjudicated by a third reviewer.

# Results
Retrieval augmentation may reduce unverifiable references in this sample. The assisted workflow
produced 3.1 unverifiable references per 1,000 words (95% CI 2.4–3.8) versus 5.7 (95% CI
4.9–6.5) for the control, a difference of 2.6 (95% CI 1.5–3.7).

# Discussion
The reduction may not generalise beyond this sample. A limitation is that only one corpus was
studied and the confidence interval includes effects near zero. The finding is consistent with
earlier reports but does not establish a causal mechanism.
